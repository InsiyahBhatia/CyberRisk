# Framework Reference Summary (project-authored)

> Project-authored plain-language summaries of public frameworks, written for the CyberRisk demo. They paraphrase publicly described concepts and are not the official text of any standard.

Framework: Multiple

Source type: public framework summary

## NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 functions

CSF 2.0 organises cybersecurity outcomes into six functions: Govern (GV), Identify (ID), Protect (PR), Detect (DE), Respond (RS) and Recover (RC). Govern covers organisational context, risk management strategy, roles, policy and supply chain risk. Identify covers asset management, risk assessment and improvement. Protect covers identity management and access control (PR.AA), awareness and training (PR.AT), data security (PR.DS), platform security (PR.PS) and technology infrastructure resilience (PR.IR). Detect covers continuous monitoring (DE.CM) and adverse event analysis (DE.AE). Respond covers incident management (RS.MA), analysis (RS.AN), communication (RS.CO) and mitigation (RS.MI). Recover covers recovery plan execution (RC.RP) and recovery communication (RC.CO).

## ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A themes

Annex A groups security controls into four themes: Organizational controls (A.5), People controls (A.6), Physical controls (A.7) and Technological controls (A.8). Technological controls include secure authentication (A.8.5), management of technical vulnerabilities (A.8.8), information backup (A.8.13), logging (A.8.15) and monitoring activities (A.8.16). Certification against ISO/IEC 27001 can only be granted by an accredited certification body after an audit.

## SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria

SOC 2 reports are issued by licensed CPA firms. The common criteria (CC) cover the control environment (CC1), communication (CC2), risk assessment (CC3), monitoring (CC4), control activities (CC5), logical and physical access (CC6), system operations (CC7), change management (CC8) and risk mitigation (CC9). Additional criteria cover availability, confidentiality, processing integrity and privacy. Internal readiness metrics are not a SOC 2 opinion.

## MITRE ATT&CK

ATT&CK is a knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques observed in the real world. Tactics describe the adversary's goal, such as Initial Access, Execution or Exfiltration. Techniques describe how the goal is achieved, such as T1566 Phishing or T1190 Exploit Public-Facing Application. ATT&CK is threat context; it is not a control framework or a compliance standard.

## CVSS and prioritisation

The Common Vulnerability Scoring System rates the technical severity of a vulnerability on a 0-10 scale. CVSS does not measure organisational risk by itself. Organisational risk also depends on asset criticality, exposure and whether the vulnerability is being actively exploited. The CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog lists vulnerabilities with evidence of exploitation in the wild; inclusion is a prioritisation signal, not proof that a particular organisation has been compromised.
